# Clayspad — image prompts

Ready to paste into Google Flow. Each one is a single descriptor chain, with a
separate negative line underneath it.

They all share one palette and one lighting setup, which is what makes six
separately generated images look like one brand instead of six images that
happen to be green.

## The palette

Paste the hex values in rather than naming the colours. "Neon green" means a
dozen different greens to a generator; `#3FE08A` means one.

| Role | Hex | Where it goes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Neon green | `#3FE08A` | The accent: glowing seams, cores, portal light |
| Green, hot core | `#A7F3C4` | The brightest point of a glow, and nothing else |
| Green, deep wash | `#0E2B21` | The bloom in the background behind a subject |
| Clay, light | `#8D949C` | Top surfaces catching the key light |
| Clay, mid | `#565D65` | The body of the material |
| Clay, dark | `#2B3037` | Undersides, cavities, contact shadow |
| Near-black | `#0B0E0D` | Background |
| Off-white | `#F2F5F4` | Wordmark, where it is not green |

## The two rules that hold the set together

**The material is clay, not metal.** Smooth, dense, slightly glossy, with soft
rounded bevels rather than machined chamfers. It reads as something shaped by
hand and then fired, which is the whole reason the project is called what it is.

**The green is emissive, never painted.** It comes out of the object — a glowing
seam, a lit core, a portal — and it is never a colour sprayed onto the surface.
A render that comes back with green *on* the clay instead of *out* of it does
not match the rest of the set.

## Notes that apply to all of them

**Spelling.** Generators drop and double letters without warning. Where a prompt
sets CLAYSPAD, read it letter by letter before you keep the result. The safer
route for anything final is to generate the art clean and set the type in an
editor, where it is spelled correctly and can be reused at any size.

**No domain and no handle in any image**, even added afterwards, until both are
registered. Hoodpad shipped a banner printing `HOODPAD.FUN` and `@HOODPAD` when
neither was registered — that is why this is a rule here and not a preference.

**No figures on any image.** Not the supply, not the split, not the fee, not a
market cap, not a percentage of any kind. Those are constants in `Clayspad.sol`
and `ClayHook.sol` and that is the only place they are published — not on the
art, not in a post, not on the site. An image outlives the conversation it was
agreed in, and a figure printed on one is a screenshot waiting to outlive the
version it was true for.

This applies to text added in an editor afterwards, not just to what the
generator draws. The banner copy in [`BANNER-COPY.md`](BANNER-COPY.md) is
already written to it.

The one thing that is not a figure: **the level line in prompt 2 is a
proportion, not a printed number.** The vessel is filled to three quarters of
its height because that is what the contract sets, and nothing on the image says
so in digits. That is fine, and it is the reason that mark is worth having.

**Sizes to export.**

| Asset | Size | Prompt |
| --- | --- | --- |
| X profile picture | 1000 × 1000 | 1A — cropped to a circle, so no wordmark |
| Anywhere the full square shows | 1000 × 1000 | 1B — with the wordmark |
| X header | 1500 × 500 | 3 — bottom-left stays clear |
| Website hero / link preview | 1200 × 630 or 1920 × 1080 | 4 |

**Two logo directions, pick one.** 1A/1B is a letterform: it reads as a brand
immediately and says nothing about the mechanism. Prompt 2 is the kiln: it says
exactly what the product does. They are not meant to coexist — generate both
once and choose.
